Wyoming generation tracker

As of 16 November 2026. Statuses are as reported to EIA for 31 December 2025 in the EIA-860 2025 data release, the most recent final data. They will be updated when EIA publishes newer data.

Summary

For 2025, developers and utilities reported 17 active proposed generators in Wyoming, totalling 7,206.6 MW, against 12,735.6 MW of existing capacity. Of that, 3,550.5 MW was under construction.

  • Onshore Wind Turbine: 4,060.6 MW
  • Solar Photovoltaic: 1,427.0 MW
  • Batteries: 1,099.0 MW
  • Nuclear: 500.0 MW
  • Natural Gas Fired Combustion Turbine: 120.0 MW

A further 5 generators (621.1 MW) were reported as indefinitely postponed or cancelled, and are listed separately below. Two existing generators report a planned retirement.

How planned coal retirement dates have changed

EIA-860 records, each year, the retirement date that a plant's owner plans for each generator. Comparing the annual editions shows how those plans have moved. Between the 2023 and 2024 editions, 10 Wyoming coal units totalling 3,656.1 MW stopped reporting a planned retirement date: Dave Johnston 1, 2 and 4, Jim Bridger 1 to 4, Naughton 1 and 2, and Wyodak. Only Dave Johnston 3 kept its date, December 2027. Two of the units, Jim Bridger 1 and 2, had been converted to burn natural gas by the 2024 edition.

Interconnection queue

Before a generator can connect to the grid, its developer must request interconnection from the transmission provider and pass through a series of studies. Berkeley Lab's Queued Up data file gathers these requests from every queue in the country. It lists 400 requests for projects in Wyoming, covering requests through the end of 2025.

At the end of 2025, 73 requests for 16,175.3 MW were active: 1.3 times Wyoming's existing capacity of 12,735.6 MW, and 2.2 times the 7,206.6 MW of proposed generators reported to EIA. A queue request is an early step, not a plan to build: 26 of the active requests (5,210.5 MW) had signed an interconnection agreement. Of the 247 Wyoming requests entered in 2020 or earlier, 48 (19%) reached commercial operation and 192 (78%) were withdrawn.

Reading this tracker

  • Every entry comes from EIA Form 860, as processed by PUDL and checked against EIA's own release. The EIA plant ID links each row to EIA's records.
  • Proposed projects and dates are reported by their developers. Many proposed projects are delayed or never built, and planned dates often move.
  • Status codes show each generator's status on 31 December 2025, using EIA's definitions: P, planned with regulatory approvals not initiated; L, approvals pending; T, approvals received; U and V, under construction (up to and above half complete); TS, built but not yet in commercial operation; IP, indefinitely postponed; CN, cancelled.
  • Battery storage paired with a solar project is listed as its own generator, as EIA reports it.
