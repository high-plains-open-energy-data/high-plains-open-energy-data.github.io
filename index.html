Open, checked data on the energy system of Wyoming and the High Plains

High Plains Open Energy Data publishes verified public data on electricity generation and transmission, tracks what is being built and retired, and provides teaching material that helps people use both. It starts with Wyoming.

Data

Datasets built from public sources, with every figure checked against its original source and every step documented so it can be reproduced.

Monitor

An independent tracker of the generating capacity proposed, under construction and planned for retirement in the region, with a source for every entry.

Teaching

Lessons and notebooks that use the same data, for students, teachers and anyone who wants to understand the region's energy system.

Why the High Plains

Wyoming produces far more electricity than it uses, and the region's generation, transmission and coal transition matter well beyond its borders. Much of the public data that describes them is scattered across federal forms and hard to use. This project brings it together for Wyoming first, checks it, and explains it in plain language for journalists, legislators, local governments, students and anyone taking part in decisions about energy projects.

Independence

The project is independent. It is not affiliated with any utility, energy company or government agency, and it takes no position for or against any project. Its aim is accurate public information.

Built on open data

The datasets are built on the Public Utility Data Liberation project (PUDL), maintained by Catalyst Cooperative, and checked against the original U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) releases. Corrections that belong upstream are reported to PUDL.
