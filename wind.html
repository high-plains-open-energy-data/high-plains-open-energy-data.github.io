Wyoming wind capacity factors, 2001 to 2025

Dataset as of 16 November 2026. Covers every wind plant in Wyoming reported to EIA, using final data through 2025.

A wind farm's capacity factor is the electricity it generated in a period divided by what it would have generated running at full nameplate capacity for the whole period. It measures how much of the time, in effect, the turbines were producing at full output, and it depends on the wind, on the turbines, and on how often they were stopped for maintenance or because the grid could not take their power.

The fleet in 2025

Wyoming had 28 wind plants with 4,707.4 MW of capacity at the end of 2025, which generated 12.73 TWh. The 25 plants that operated all year at unchanged capacity, 79% of the capacity, had a combined capacity factor of 35.3%. Plants that entered service in 2020 or later (11 plants) reached 38.1% together, against 30.5% for the 13 plants that entered service in 2010 or earlier.

Month by month

Wyoming's wind farms generate most in winter. Over 2016 to 2025, the fleet's capacity factor was highest in December (49.0%) and lowest in July (21.4%).

Which plant-years are counted

A plant-year is counted only if every wind generator at the plant ran for the whole year at the same nameplate capacity as the year before. A plant's first year is partial, and a year in which turbines were added, retired or replaced would divide a year's generation by the wrong capacity, so those years are kept in the data with the reason but given no capacity factor. Of 437 plant-years from 2001 to 2025, 370 are counted; the rest are 31 generator entered service during year, 19 capacity changed during year, 7 no net generation reported, 5 no capacity record for the previous year, 5 generator retired during year. 2001 itself has no capacity factor because the data begin that year, so no plant has a record for the year before. Fleet figures are the total generation of the counted plants divided by their total possible generation, so large plants weigh more than small ones.

Checks

  • Every Wyoming wind plant's 2025 net generation, for the year and for each month, matches EIA's own EIA-923 workbook to the megawatt-hour (27 plants, 12.73 TWh).
  • For every plant-year, the monthly generation adds up to the annual figure (largest difference 0 MWh).
  • For the 335 counted plant-years since 2008, PUDL's generator-level generation agrees with the plant-level figure (largest relative difference 0).
  • Capacity and generation for 2025 agree with the 2025 generation dataset.

Notes and limitations

  • A low capacity factor is not by itself a sign of a poor site or poor turbines. EIA-923 does not report curtailment, outages or the wind resource, so this data cannot separate them.
  • Nameplate capacity is as reported to EIA-860. A plant repowered with new turbines at unchanged nameplate capacity cannot be detected from these forms, so the year of the work may be counted.
  • 4 plants (Casper Wind Farm, Foote Creek II, Foote Creek III, Foote Creek IV) reported no wind generation in 7 plant-years while still listed as existing; those years are not counted.

Downloads

  • wyoming_wind_plant_months.csv: one row for each plant and month of every counted plant-year.
  • wyoming_wind_fleet.csv: the fleet figures above, by year and by calendar month.
